How to Write a News Story

Time: 30-90 minutes
Awesome rating: 100% awesome

As a journalist, writing a news story is such a good skill to have. AP style, interviewing, and just good writing can help a TON in the professional world! Follow these steps to learn how to write a great story.

  • Things You'll Need

  • Computer with a Document Editor
  • A voice recording app OR a notepad
  • An AP Stylebook (optiona)

  1. Choose a newsworthy topic you care about.

    Writing news doesn't always have to be boring. Find something interesting to write about and ask people about.

  2. Do your research and take notes.

    Objectivity is one of the most important parts of journalism. Before you start writing, take away your biases and find credible sources.

    An image of a pen and notebook with writing inside.

  3. Find and interview relevant sources.

    Whether or not you agree with the people you interview, finding relevant people can help your story be stronger. People who work in a certain industry, near a certain place, or were around at a certain time are all examples of relevant sources.

    A man interviewing someone on the street.

  4. Answer the five Ws for your story.

    When beginning your story, think about the five Ws: who, what, where, when, and why. This will give you some grounds for a good lead.

  5. Create an outline using the inverted pyramid structure.

    The inverted pyramid is a technique where you pu the most important information first. This is a good practice for newswriting. Always make sure that the stuff people want to hear is at the top.

  6. Write a strong lead.

    Writing a strong lead can really help enhance your story. You should always start with a "what" or "why" for the most effective lead. Take a look at this video for more help.

  7. Add crucial content with a nut graph.

    After your lead, you should follow up with a nut graph. This should provide context for your story; any added detail is encouraged.

  8. Draft the body of your article.

    Most news writing is done in a copy-quote pattern that generally works well for the story. Use the interviews you've gathered and an unbiased style to keep this formula going.

  9. Include quotes and attributions throughout your piece.

    A good news story features a lot of quotes and many, many attributions. Make sure to put the person's full name, along with their title for a first=time introduction. Having a lot of different quotes can give your story character. Ex. "I really like the playground," Jerry Bennett, a second-grader at Johnsonville Elementary School said.

  10. Copy edit, fact-check, and revise your initial draft.

    Associated Press (AP) style is a necessary part of journalistic writing. Make sure that everything follows AP style. Make sure that all your facts are correct, and that you don't make up quotes. These small details can really impact your story.
